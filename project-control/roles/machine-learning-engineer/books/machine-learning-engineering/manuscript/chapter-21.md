## MLE-CH-21-S02 — Procedure: Falsify Before You Generalize

**Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-061.** A hostile dossier review recomputes identities, links, limitations, authority records, five-port invariants, recovery paths, and terminal non-serving evidence, then attempts to falsify each claimed state. Checklist presence without adversarial evidence testing is insufficient. Recompute rather than trust a display value. Reverse a source-to-claim link; remove a limitation; swap a prior hash; privilege one port; remove an authority record; revive a residual route; and verify that the claimed state no longer passes. MLE-BSRC-007, MLE-BSRC-027, MLE-BSRC-036, and MLE-BSRC-039 support test, lifecycle, provenance, and control-review context, not specialist approval replacement.

**Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-062.** Systemic learning turns repeated evidence failures into a narrowly scoped reusable standard: named problem, affected artifacts, validation rule, proposal owner, adoption authority, acceptance evidence, and follow-up measure. The MLE may propose and mentor through evidence but cannot impose cross-team policy. A standard proposal that lacks an adopter is an observation, not adopted policy. A generic slogan like “improve governance” is not a standard because it cannot be tested against an artifact.

Its canonical edges are MLE-BSRC-027 for contextual governance, MLE-BSRC-030 for reusable secure-development practice, and MLE-BSRC-041 for learning/action ownership. None makes a proposal self-adopting.

**Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-063.** System-level ML evidence widens beyond the model to data dependencies, consumers, feedback loops, release, operation, recovery, controls, and retirement. Reusable tests expose this debt across a workload. They must not turn model novelty, tool count, a title ladder, or generalized platform ownership into evidence of readiness. MLE-BSRC-005 and MLE-BSRC-007 support system debt and production-readiness categories, not an organization-specific seniority rubric.

The review procedure starts with bytes and moves outward. Recompute every artifact hash and input binding, follow forward and reverse source/claim/case edges, inspect limitation and authority fields, verify recovery identity, then challenge BL-19’s inventory and observation-time bound. Apply stale-hash, broken-link, missing-owner, privileged-port, silent-repair, residual-serving, and self-adoption mutations one at a time so each diagnostic is discriminating. A local defect gets a new repair identity and preserved history. Only a repeated pattern justifies a systemic proposal, and that proposal must name affected artifacts, a validation rule, proposal owner, adoption authority, acceptance evidence, and follow-up measure.

Choose assertions that would be inconvenient to lose. Recompute a displayed digest from its bound bytes; follow a bibliography entry to the exact claim and limitation; reverse a case edge; inspect the prior-good identity; and challenge RETIRED with a known fallback route. Each failure must explain its decision consequence. A stale hash breaks identity, a missing limitation broadens a claim, a missing authority makes a decision unowned, and a residual path contradicts terminal state. That explanation prevents a prose-only repair.

The strongest conclusion is bounded: the specified dossier was independently challenged against its frozen evidence and its failures, limitations, authority routes, port invariants, recovery chain, and non-serving proof. Limitations protect the closing claim. Review evidence cannot replace domain, safety, privacy, legal, security, product, or organization adoption authority. A reusable standard may be proposed but has no binding force until its actual owners accept it. The chapter does not claim production readiness, career seniority, policy compliance, or business impact.

Prior failures remain part of the evidence. The hostile reviewer preserves the original mutation, disposition, limitation, owner route, repair identity, and reacceptance condition; it does not portray a repaired defect as still current or erase how the repair was earned. This history distinguishes a changed candidate from a clearer explanation and lets a later reviewer verify that the failure condition was actually removed. A perfect-looking final register with no failure trail is weaker than a bounded record that explains its corrections.

## MLE-CH-21-S05 — Failure Labs

`MLE-CH-21-LAB-01` runs a synthetic-deterministic `verify-dossier` fixture that recomputes hashes, reverse links, limitations, authority records, port shape, recovery, and BL-19 non-serving evidence. `FIX-MLE-CH-21-1` is the closed complete-dossier input; `FIX-MLE-CH-21-2` is the closed mutation input. Inputs are BL-00 through BL-19, their exact identities and links, limitations, authorities, five-port records, recovery, retirement inventory, observation time, and proposal fields only. Legal dispositions are PASS, HOLD, REJECT, or REOPEN. Named diagnostics are “stale hash,” “broken reverse link,” “missing authority,” “privileged port depth,” “residual serving state,” and “self-adopted standard.” Acceptance requires the full chain, preserved failure history, external adoption route, and no illegal state repair. Prohibited effects are network, shell, provider, credential, model, production, policy, publication, and authority mutation.

The important result is not a universal verifier score. It is that a reviewer can describe why a state failed, what evidence would correct it, and who may decide the correction. A standard that cannot expose such a failure has not yet earned reuse.

The systemic-proposal mutation tests another boundary. A recurring temporal-identity gap may justify a validation rule, but the fixture fails if the proposal owner also writes “adopted.” The expected record lists affected artifacts, the rule, acceptance evidence, adoption authority, and follow-up measure, then stops at routed. This makes mentoring practical—another engineer can apply the rule and interpret failure—without turning expertise into policy power.

## MLE-CH-21-S06 — Five-Port Transfer

`PORT-MANAGED`, `PORT-CLASSICAL`, `PORT-DEEP`, `PORT-EDGE`, and `PORT-SHARED` receive equal hostile review: recompute identity and links, inspect limitations and authority, test recovery, verify terminal non-serving evidence, and reject special pleading. Managed provider mechanics cannot define the standard. Classical simplicity cannot waive evidence. Deep novelty cannot count as readiness. Edge constraints cannot erase delayed-path limits. Shared workload review cannot become fleet ownership. Equal ports prevent a tool or architecture from becoming an implicit approval hierarchy.

Managed requires exportable identities and limitations; an opaque-provider mutation routes to provider/platform owners and no provider claim establishes qualification. Classical requires feature, preprocessing, estimator, consumer, and recovery links; mismatch routes to data/evaluation and simplicity grants no waiver. Deep requires run, checkpoint, preprocessor, dependency, runtime, and recovery identity; wrong identity routes to research/platform and novelty grants no status. Edge requires device inventory, package, delayed path, rollback, and recheck; disconnection routes to hardware/operations/domain and fictional evidence grants no safety. Shared requires tenant/workload identity, compatibility, controls, recovery, and fleet separation; collision or fleet pressure routes to platform/SRE/security and workload review is not fleet assurance. Every row returns the same bounded hostile-review result.

Apply one proposed rule to all five records and inspect failure behavior, not tool similarity. The managed row may fail because an identity cannot be exported; the classical row because preprocessing lacks a reverse link; the deep row because a checkpoint is privileged; the edge row because a delayed device has no recheck; and the shared row because tenant evidence is mistaken for fleet authority. A reusable standard reports each mechanism-specific failure through the same decision, evidence, limitation, owner, and next-route interface. If it needs one vendor or model class to pass, it is a mechanism guide rather than a workload standard.

The comparison also tests restraint. A stronger deep-port record does not raise the other ports’ authority, and an opaque managed port does not lower their proof burden. Each missing field stays visible. The proposal can recommend a shared validator for these semantics, but only the named organization owner can decide adoption, rollout, resourcing, or policy effect.

## MLE-CH-21-S07 — Assessment and Qualification Gate

`MLE-CH-21-ASMT-01` gives the reader a dossier with one stale hash, one missing limitation, one MLE-signed formal decision, one privileged deep-port rule, one residual alias, and a vague cross-team improvement idea. The reader must falsify each state, route the local repair, and rewrite the improvement as a narrow proposal with problem, artifacts, validation rule, owner, adoption authority, evidence, and follow-up. A correct answer keeps RETIRED before REVIEWED and refuses self-adoption.

**Answer intent:** explain each mutation’s decision consequence, repair only the local record with preserved history, convert the repeated gap into a testable proposal, route adoption externally, reject the residual route and privileged port, and keep RETIRED before REVIEWED.

A correct response states “not established” where evidence is absent rather than manufacturing a field. It shows the new local repair identity, preserves the old failure, and demonstrates that the repaired graph recomputes. It rewrites “improve governance” into a narrow validation rule with named inputs and observable failure. Finally, it identifies the organization owner who may adopt that rule and leaves the proposal pending; technical clarity is not adoption authority.

### Qualification Gate

Issue BL-20 only when the hostile review recomputes and challenges identities, links, limitations, owners, five-port invariants, recovery, and terminal retirement evidence; the state is already RETIRED; local repairs preserve history; and any reusable standard has an external adoption authority. Otherwise HOLD, REJECT, or REOPEN. PASS establishes REVIEWED for the private dossier, not policy adoption or a new body of work.
