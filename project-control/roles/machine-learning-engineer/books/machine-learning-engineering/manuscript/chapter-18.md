## MLE-CH-18-S01 — Decision and Bench Setup

### Bench Setup

BL-16 records observations, including uncertainty. This chapter asks what happens when an observation or incident requires containment: who commands the incident, what exact state can be restored, what changes create a new candidate, and what evidence returns through qualification? The incoming state is OBSERVED. BL-17 may lead to REQUALIFIED or ROLLED-BACK; it never treats restoration as closure. The Bench Sheet fixes incident identity, detection time, observed impact, containment action, previous-good identity, changed-evidence record, retained authorities, follow-up, and limitation.

The MLE owns workload-specific diagnosis, evidence change, and repair. Operations owns incident command and fleet action. Evaluation and formal risk owners retain their gates. The **state and dossier delta** is `OBSERVED → REQUALIFIED|ROLLED-BACK`; the **authority route** prevents a technical contributor from declaring an incident closed. The **next evidence** is a controlled dossier or an earlier re-entry, depending on what changed.

Traceability is explicit: architecture `MLE-CLM-003`, `MLE-CLM-013`, `MLE-CLM-016`, `MLE-CLM-017`, `MLE-CLM-018`, `MLE-CLM-019`, and `MLE-CLM-022`; boundaries `BND-05`, `BND-09`, `BND-10`, `BND-11`, `BND-12`, and `BND-14`; scenarios `SCN-01`, `SCN-04`, `SCN-07`, and `SCN-09`; domain `PD-11`. The architecture set binds operational identity, uncertainty, change, recovery, limitation, and disposition. The boundaries separate evaluation, incident command, shared mechanisms, security, and formal acceptance; the scenarios stress consequential failure, observation, edge recovery, and candidate change; the domain names recovery and continuous controls. These assignments do not duplicate primary teaching.

## MLE-CH-18-S02 — Procedure: Containment Is Not Qualification

**Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-052.** During a model-related incident, incident command and fleet action stay with the named operations authority. The MLE supplies workload diagnosis, evidence change, and repair. A useful first record distinguishes observed signal, known impact, unknown impact, immediate containment, and the owners who can decide broader action. This avoids two failures: an MLE silently taking fleet authority, or an incident commander treating a generic rollback as evidence that the workload is understood. MLE-BSRC-039, MLE-BSRC-040, and MLE-BSRC-041 support operational and learning context, not appointment of local roles.

The canonical claim edges are MLE-BSRC-027 for contextual governance, MLE-BSRC-031 for bounded control context, and MLE-BSRC-042 for an identity-sensitive public mechanism. They support separation of evidence and authority, not a local incident-command appointment.

**Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-053.** Rollback contains exposure by returning deployment to a named previous-good state. It does not establish incident closure or qualify a repair. The previous-good target must be the immutable BL-14/BL-15 identity, not a mutable alias. If code, data, features, model, runtime, credentials, consumer contract, or evaluation basis changes, allocate a new candidate identity and return to applicable technical and external gates. A platform rollback may restore only a deployment template; the dossier must separately account for other state. MLE-BSRC-016 illustrates mechanics, while MLE-BSRC-027 and MLE-BSRC-031 preserve lifecycle and control context.

The exact edge set is MLE-BSRC-007 for re-testing discipline, MLE-BSRC-016 for rollback mechanics, MLE-BSRC-027 for lifecycle context, and MLE-BSRC-040 for staged recovery. No edge makes restoration equal qualification.

**Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-054.** A post-incident record binds observed impact, detection, containment, recovery, causal evidence, contributing conditions, corrective actions, owners, and follow-up verification. It is not proof that corrective actions completed or that a repaired model is qualified. The record therefore separates hypothesis from established causal evidence and assigns due evidence, not just due dates. Restoration without learning leaves recurrence risk untested; learning without verification is only a proposal.

Its canonical edges are MLE-BSRC-030 for secure-development follow-through, MLE-BSRC-039 for bounded incident/control evidence, and MLE-BSRC-041 for learning and action ownership. None proves action completion or risk acceptance.

Run the procedure as a chronology. Preserve the BL-16 observation and its uncertainty; record who accepted command; record the containment requested and the action actually observed; resolve the immutable prior-good identity; enumerate consumers, caches, credentials, data transformations, and devices not proved restored; then create a changed-evidence ledger. For every change, state which old evidence remains applicable, which evidence is superseded, the new candidate identity, and the gate and owner that must reconsider it. This makes parallel repair tracks visible without collapsing build, data, consumer, security, evaluation, and operations authority into “fixed.”

Limitations remain explicit. No organization-specific escalation path is prescribed. A rollback cannot prove data, feature, credential, consumer, or evaluation recovery. A postmortem cannot authorize release or show actions completed. The MLE routes evidence; operations, product, domain, evaluation, security, and formal owners retain their decisions.

Incident records need a strict chronology. Capture what was detected, by which signal, at what time; what was known and unknown; what containment was requested and executed; which prior-good identity was selected; and what evidence changed after recovery. This chronology prevents an after-the-fact explanation from becoming falsely certain. A hypothesis can help diagnosis, but must remain a hypothesis until causal evidence supports it. The record can evolve without rewriting the original operational decision.

The recovery test is narrower than it sounds. It asks whether the named route can return the workload to the stated prior-good state under the fixture. It does not ask whether every dependency, consumer, cache, credential, data transformation, or human decision has returned to a safe condition. List those seams. If a rollback only changes a deployment template, say so. If a consumer migration is pending, say so. These limitations make a decisive action usable without overclaiming.

Changed evidence needs a ledger. For each repair, record what changed, why, which old evidence is no longer applicable, which states it re-enters, and which owners must review it. A new model file is obvious; a changed feature order, runtime dependency, interface contract, dataset slice, or permitted use may be equally material. The ledger does not decree every rule; it preserves information for existing gates and their owners.

## MLE-CH-18-S06 — Five-Port Transfer

Across `PORT-MANAGED`, `PORT-CLASSICAL`, `PORT-DEEP`, `PORT-EDGE`, and `PORT-SHARED`, the invariant is the same: identify incident evidence, contain through the named owner, return to an immutable previous-good state where applicable, allocate a new identity for changed evidence, and requalify before renewed exposure. Managed tooling may change export mechanics; classical paths may emphasize preprocessing; deep paths may emphasize checkpoint/runtime identity; edge paths may include disconnected devices; shared paths must preserve tenant/fleet separation. No port makes rollback equal release authorization.

Each row is mechanically testable. Managed requires exportable provider action and recovery identities; unavailable export routes to provider/platform owners, and provider status is not qualification. Classical requires feature order, preprocessing, estimator, and rollback evidence; a mismatch routes to data/evaluation owners, and simplicity is no waiver. Deep requires run, checkpoint, preprocessor, hardware context, and recovery identity; a wrong identity routes to research/platform owners, and scale proves nothing. Edge requires device inventory, package, connectivity, rollback, and delayed recheck; a disconnected or stale device routes to hardware/operations/domain owners, and fictional Benchline evidence implies no safety. Shared requires tenant/workload identity, compatibility, recovery, and fleet separation; tenant collision or fleet pressure routes to platform/SRE/security, and workload recovery is not fleet assurance. Every row returns only the bounded BL-17 disposition.

## MLE-CH-18-S07 — Assessment and Qualification Gate

`MLE-CH-18-ASMT-01` presents a restored service, a changed preprocessing artifact, an unknown consumer effect, and an MLE-signed closure note. The reader must identify the prior-good identity, mark the new candidate requirement, preserve unknowns, assign incident command and qualification owners, and refuse closure. A correct answer differentiates containment, recovery, causal learning, and requalification.

**Answer intent:** distinguish observation, containment, rollback, repair, and requalification; preserve unknown consumers; allocate a new identity for changed evidence; route command and formal gates externally; refuse the MLE-signed closure.

The response is complete only when it identifies what the rollback actually restored and what remained outside that action. It must keep the changed preprocessor under a new candidate, state which earlier compatibility and evaluation evidence no longer applies, and name the gate that receives the new delta. The unknown consumer remains an unknown with an owner and next check. “Service restored” is therefore evidence of one action, not a substitute for recovery scope, causal learning, or external requalification.

### Qualification Gate

Issue BL-17 only when the record has observed impact and uncertainty, detection, containment, immutable recovery identity, changed-evidence handling, causal-evidence status, corrective-action owners, follow-up verification, limitation, and authority routes. Otherwise HOLD, REJECT, or REOPEN. A ROLLED-BACK state is controlled exposure reduction, not incident closure; REQUALIFIED requires applicable evidence and external gates.

## MLE-CH-18-S08 — Durable Handoff

BL-17 binds the observation from BL-16 to incident record, containment, recovery target, changes, re-entry gates, limitation, source notes, currentness triggers, and owners. It hands either a REQUALIFIED or ROLLED-BACK state to controls without self-approving policy or release. Any changed purpose, data, labels, features, runtime, dependencies, interface, serving envelope, authority, constraint, or permitted use reopens the appropriate state. This private chapter stops before operational execution, publication, image generation, or next-role work.

The durable record states whether the incident began from a signal or established outcome, what uncertainty remains, whether containment covered the named scope, which candidate identity now exists, which earlier evidence still applies, and which owner must act next. It stops when that bounded record is complete; recovery evidence stays visible, unknowns stay visible, and renewed exposure waits for the applicable external gate.
