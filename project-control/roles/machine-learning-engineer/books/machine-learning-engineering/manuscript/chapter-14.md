# MLE-CH-14 — Bind Interfaces, Consumers, and Compatibility
A model release is unsafe when its consumers, interfaces, compatibility window, or fallback owner are implicit.
This chapter advances the private Benchline dossier from BL-12 to BL-13. ## MLE-CH-14-S03 — Evidence interpretation

A compatibility result is indexed by producer bytes, format or operator version, runtime, consumer identity, interface meaning, fixture, and time window. Remove one coordinate and “compatible” loses a stable subject. The MLE validates workload compatibility but cannot own every consuming application or agent effect. BL-13 records bounded evidence for known consumers and routes the remaining application decisions to their owners rather than extrapolating from a checker.
The revealing counterexample is a response that preserves field names and types but changes a score from a fraction to a percentage. Structural validation passes, while an old consumer silently applies the wrong threshold. The drill must catch the semantic mismatch through the legacy fixture, preserve the former contract, and route migration or rollback to that application's owner. Updating the registry to “green” without exercising the consumer would hide precisely the dependency the chapter is designed to expose.

In the successful drill, old and successor consumers run against their declared contracts, coexistence dates are explicit, and each fallback resolves to an owner and previous-good artifact. In the failed drill, the schema checker remains green but a semantic-unit mutation breaks the legacy consumer and its owner is missing. BL-13 must retain the failure and block deprecation. The evidence points to a practical recovery: restore the old semantics, version the break and migrate the client, or keep both paths available until the owner accepts the transition.

The chapter-specific rechecks travel with the record: compatibility evidence obligations bind producer bytes, format and operator versions, runtime, interface semantics, known consumer identities, old/new fixtures, coexistence window, fallback owners, legal state, and next migration proof.
