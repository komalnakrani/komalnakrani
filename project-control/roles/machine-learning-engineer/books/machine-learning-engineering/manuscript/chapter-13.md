Repair issues a new BL-12 identity after the content-addressed graph is rebuilt; it never edits the old record. This test supports only mechanical provenance integrity. It cannot decide workload suitability, approve a release, or authorize any security, platform, product, domain, or evaluation action.

A tag can help a human find a candidate, but it cannot carry the evidentiary weight of the candidate's digest. The distinction becomes obvious when two investigators resolve the same tag at different times and receive different artifacts. BL-12 avoids that ambiguity by storing the resolved digest as the release identity while retaining the tag only as optional discovery metadata. The same discipline applies to approval references: a ticket number locates a decision record but does not reveal its scope unless that record is resolved and bound. When either link is incomplete, the honest response is to hold the package, restore the missing edge, and rerun dependent checks under a new manifest identity.
SLSA provenance is inspected as a set of connected assertions, not as a badge. The examiner verifies that the subject digest equals the packaged artifact, that the identified build inputs resolve, and that the attestation belongs to the expected builder and process. A valid envelope whose subject points elsewhere is a failure; so is a correct subject with an absent source or build edge. The frozen fixture makes those distinctions deterministic and records which assertion produced RED. SLSA 1.2 supplies a versioned supply-chain mechanism, while BL-12 supplies the workload-specific binding and limitations. Neither object demonstrates model quality or confers permission to release, so those judgments stay with their named custodians.

The provenance graph also clarifies how a build engineer and an evaluation owner may reach different but compatible conclusions. The build engineer can confirm that an attestation binds the expected artifact to identified inputs. The evaluation owner may still refuse qualification because the report omits a consequential segment or evaluated bound. BL-12 preserves both facts instead of allowing the successful supply-chain check to overwrite the unresolved evaluation issue. Its owner fields route the mechanical defect to the build or platform custodian and the evidentiary gap to evaluation or domain authority. That division turns disagreement into two inspectable work items and prevents a technically correct attestation from being presented as an all-purpose approval.

### PORT-SHARED
On PORT-SHARED, equal release evidence is expressed through multi-tenant features, training, registry, serving, and observability. Component inventory exposes a different problem on each port. The managed route may hide provider-side layers; the classical route can omit a preprocessing library; the deep route must bind checkpoint, tokenizer, and accelerator context; the edge route needs device-runtime components; and the shared route must separate tenant dependencies from fleet dependencies. Port parity does not require identical inventory formats. It requires every adapter to state what its inventory covers, connect that inventory to the exact executable, and retain gaps as limitations. BL-12 therefore compares resolvability and scope rather than file shape. An opaque export, missing transform, or unbound shared component stays visible and routes to its external owner instead of being excused by the convenience of a particular packaging mechanism.

The Benchline inventory fixture contains a small, fixed component set whose relationship to the executable is known in advance. Its positive path confirms that every declared component and the package digest can be resolved together. The negative path removes one lineage edge without changing the loadability check, forcing `LINEAGE_MISSING`. This exercise demonstrates that an inventory is tested for binding, not merely for existence. When transferred to another port, the component names and tooling may change, but the examiner still needs a reproducible way to detect an omitted dependency. Because the records are synthetic, success establishes only the mechanics of the check; it says nothing about real vulnerability status, licensing, performance, safety, or operational approval.

At publication freeze, the examiner checks whether any cited specification has been superseded and whether its fields still mean what the release manifest claims. A newer document does not retroactively invalidate historical BL-12 bytes, but it may require a new verification before reuse. The stable rule is that component, subject, and provenance links resolve against the artifact. The volatile details are schemas, tooling behavior, and support matrices. Recording both prevents a format upgrade from silently changing the meaning of a previously accepted release graph.

The transfer review compares five concrete failure surfaces. Can the managed export be independently resolved? Does the classical package bind preprocessing order? Does the deep package identify its checkpoint and tokenizer? Does the edge package identify the device runtime? Does the shared package separate workload and platform components? For every answer, the reviewer records the artifact identity, observed verification, surviving limitation, and external owner. A PASS on one adapter cannot fill a gap on another. If a port changes its packaging tool or runtime, its prior evidence remains historical and that port alone is rechecked unless a shared dependency invalidates more of the graph. This keeps parity focused on the release contract rather than superficial sameness.
