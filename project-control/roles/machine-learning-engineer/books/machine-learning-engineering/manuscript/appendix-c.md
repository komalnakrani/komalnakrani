# Appendix C — Five-port transfer matrix

The five ports are equal mechanical adapters. They do not define five levels of
rigor and no provider is privileged. Every port carries the same decision,
disposition, evidence-envelope fields, limitations, external authority, and
dossier sequence. The mechanism and characteristic failure injection in the
table are reader-facing transfer exercises, not five distinct executable
implementations.

| Port | Variable mechanism | Evidence that must resolve | Characteristic failure injection | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Managed | Provider-managed training, registry, serving, and observation | Exportable code/data/run/package/interface/evaluation/observation/recovery identities and provider limits | Opaque provider state or unavailable export | Provider claims do not qualify the workload |
| Classical | Local transformations and statistical or classical estimator | Feature order, preprocessing, estimator, calibration/segment evidence where applicable, package, envelope, and recovery | Feature-order or preprocessing mismatch | Simplicity does not remove lifecycle evidence |
| Deep | Parameterized training, accelerator context, checkpoint, and tensor-serving path | Data, run, checkpoint, preprocessor/tokenizer, framework, driver/hardware context, evaluation, envelope, and recovery | Wrong checkpoint, preprocessing, or hardware-context identity | Scale and benchmark novelty do not replace evidence |
| Edge | Sensor or image input, constrained runtime, device package, and delayed feedback | Sensor/data/label identity, device/runtime package, measured envelope, delayed-path observation, rollback, controls, and retirement | Sensor shift, resource pressure, stale or unreachable device package | The Benchline case is fictional and proves no industrial performance or safety |
| Shared platform | Multi-tenant features, training, registry, serving, and observability | Tenant/workload identity, compatibility, resource envelope, control use, recovery, and owner routes | Tenant collision, shared-runtime upgrade, or fleet pressure | Workload evidence cannot claim platform-wide assurance |

## Invariant decision envelope

At each chapter, compare the ports by one question: would changing the
mechanism alter who decides, the evidence minimum, the legal transition, or the
limitation? If yes, the adapter has leaked authority. A managed dashboard cannot
replace exportable evidence; a local script cannot waive independent
evaluation; an accelerator cannot broaden a reproducibility claim; an edge
device cannot hide an unreachable retirement path; and a shared platform cannot
convert tenant evidence into fleet approval.

The five common decision fields are the chapter gate, incoming/outgoing state,
disposition, authority route, and next evidence. Common record fields are
defined in [Appendix A](appendix-a.md). Each part gate includes five checks—one
per port—so the complete book contains thirty-five port-aware part-exit checks.

## Companion procedure

Run `npm test` in the companion directory to verify that all five
adapters return enumerable decision and evidence fields with identical shapes
for the fixed fixtures. The forty-two named lab variants are conceptual
chapter exercises. The companion binds their labels, immutable fixture hashes,
closed schema, named diagnostic, state/hash mechanics, and equal port interface;
it does not execute the provider, estimator, accelerator, device, tenant, data,
model, evaluation, serving, observation, or retirement mechanisms described by
those variants. A programmatic `runPort(port, options)` call must target
a fresh real directory outside the repository. It performs no network, shell,
cloud, model, database, accelerator, or secret access. Successful equality
demonstrates adapter-contract mechanics only; it does not prove that a real
provider export is complete, a local environment is controlled, hardware is
equivalent, a device fleet is reachable, or a shared service is reliable.

When porting a real workload, retain the same dossier milestone IDs and record a
new mechanism-specific identity. Do not copy the synthetic expected records or
their dispositions into a production evidence store.
