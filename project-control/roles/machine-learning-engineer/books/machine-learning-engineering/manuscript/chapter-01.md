## MLE-CH-01-S01 — Decision and Bench Setup

### Bench Setup

The first decision in machine learning engineering is not which model to train. It is what, exactly, has been placed on the bench. A request may arrive as a binary file, a notebook, a tracker link, a promising chart, or a hurried message asking for “production.” None of those objects establishes a learning workload. A workload includes the intended decision, the population and conditions in which that decision matters, the evidence already available, the people who own consequential judgments, and the unresolved gaps that prevent the next state. This chapter therefore begins with an orientation test: can another reviewer identify the workload, its strongest evidenced state, and the owners of every decision without relying on the MLE’s title or private context?

Benchline begins as `BL-ENTRY`, a synthetic request containing `model.bin`, tracker run `run-019`, an ambiguous inspection request, and an incomplete owner registry. Its state is `UNORIENTED`. The file might be executable and the run might contain useful measurements, but neither artifact names the intended use, excluded use, domain-validity owner, release authority, or evidence state. Treating the file as a candidate would silently promote it. Treating the engineer as the missing authority would silently transfer decisions that belong elsewhere. The correct starting posture is curiosity with a fail-closed boundary.

The decision is to locate the workload-specific MLE responsibility inside the full lifecycle and authority map. The evidence includes immutable artifact identities, the request, the tracker record, the owner registry, and accepted claims `MLE-BCLM-001`, `MLE-BCLM-002`, and `MLE-BCLM-003`. The state and dossier delta is `UNORIENTED` to `ORIENTED`, recorded as `BL-00` version 1.0.0. The authority route sends purpose and priority to product, domain validity to a named domain owner, evaluation sufficiency to an independent evaluator, and formal safety, privacy, legal, security, platform, release, and business decisions to their retained owners. The next evidence is an approved bounded purpose plus the people authorized to judge it.

### Bench Sheet

| Field | Reader record |
|---|---|
| decision | PASS, HOLD, REJECT, or REOPEN, with a one-sentence reason |
| evidence | Exact input identities, observed gaps, limits, and hashes |
| state and dossier delta | `UNORIENTED` → `ORIENTED`; create `BL-00` |
| authority route | Route every missing consequential decision to its named external owner |
| next evidence | Approved purpose and complete owner-and-decision map |

The six recurring elements are deliberately plain: Bench Setup, decision, evidence, state and dossier delta, authority route, and next evidence. They prevent a technically impressive object from becoming the organizing truth. A model may change many times; the decision and its evidence must remain reviewable.

The frozen architecture makes that bench boundary auditable. `MLE-CLM-001` and `MLE-CLM-007` treat title and seniority as dated signals, never as proof of scope; `MLE-CLM-002`, `MLE-CLM-005`, and `MLE-CLM-006` connect workload responsibility, architecture contact, and partner translation; `MLE-CLM-021` and `MLE-CLM-022` preserve adjacent decision centers and evidence-based professional depth. The authority route is equally explicit. `BND-01`, `BND-02`, `BND-03`, `BND-04`, `BND-05`, `BND-06`, `BND-07`, and `BND-08` keep data science, data engineering, research, evaluation, product-AI, LLM, and agent specializations outside this chapter’s decision center. `BND-09`, `BND-10`, `BND-11`, and `BND-12` route reusable automation, ML platforms, software services, fleet reliability, and incident command to their owners. `BND-13`, `BND-14`, `BND-15`, `BND-16`, and `BND-17` reserve product value, security acceptance, safety authorization, privacy/legal determinations, and domain validity. These are not disclaimers after the fact: a missing routed owner is a failed orientation check. `PD-01` names the domain contract—purpose, population, incumbent, consequence, and authority must be bounded before implementation—and `BL-00` records only the first orientation step toward it.

## MLE-CH-01-S02 — Procedure: orient the workload

### Source notes and currentness: orientation evidence

Orientation is an ordered procedure, not a meeting summary. First, allocate immutable identifiers to the request, every supplied artifact, and the emerging workload. An identifier is not a filename alone. It records the artifact kind, version, content hash, source or parent, owner, limitations, and supersession relationship. If two reviewers can point to different bytes while saying “the model,” the workload is not yet oriented. `BL-ENTRY` therefore retains the binary identity and tracker identity separately; neither is allowed to stand for the entire workload.

Second, classify the strongest evidenced lifecycle state. A model file proves that bytes exist. A tracked run proves that some facts were recorded. A candidate additionally requires a retained baseline, a controlled comparison, and a nomination record. A technically qualified workload requires an accepted evaluation disposition. A released workload binds the executable to its evidence and approvals. An operating workload adds a measured serving envelope and observation evidence. The labels MODEL FILE, TRACKED RUN, CANDIDATE, QUALIFIED, RELEASED, and OPERATING are not a conveyor belt. Each is a claim with prerequisites, and missing evidence stops promotion.

Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-001: current employer postings show that the exact Machine Learning Engineer title can include training, deployment, monitoring, data quality, and iteration, but a title is volatile market evidence rather than technical doctrine. Blissway and Sprinter Health postings, `MLE-BSRC-045` and `MLE-BSRC-046`, establish only a bounded, dated signal that employers currently use the title across more than one setting. They do not establish prevalence, a universal scope, or decision authority. The practical test is artifact based: ask what evidence the engineer must produce and which decision remains external. A staff title cannot sign a domain-validity decision merely because the organization expects broad ownership.

Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-002: the supportable unit is the learning workload, whose data dependencies, pipeline behavior, tests, release state, and monitoring evidence can be inspected together. Hidden Technical Debt and the ML Test Score, `MLE-BSRC-005` and `MLE-BSRC-007`, identify failure categories extending beyond offline metrics. They do not create a universal readiness score. A workload view keeps a good model from concealing an unknown feature dependency, an undeclared consumer, an incomplete release path, or a missing observation contract. It also keeps a comprehensive platform from being mistaken for qualification of every workload using it.

Primary teaching MLE-BCLM-003: lifecycle responsibilities and communication lines must be explicit across technical and nontechnical actors. NIST AI RMF 1.0 and its Core, `MLE-BSRC-028` and `MLE-BSRC-027`, support explicit roles, responsibilities, context, and risk communication. They do not assign the MLE title to a formal authority or certify this dossier. The MLE can inventory evidence, expose gaps, implement tests, and write a technical disposition. Product still owns purpose, domain specialists still own domain validity, evaluation retains independent gate adequacy, and security, safety, privacy, legal, governance, platform, release, and business owners retain their decisions.

The procedure works best when the team separates nouns that are often collapsed. A model is a learned artifact or callable behavior. A run is one recorded execution. A candidate is a specifically nominated workload configuration. A workload is the continuing decision system whose evidence survives changes to artifacts and mechanisms. A platform is a shared capability used by many workloads. A product is the user-facing or operational context in which a purpose is pursued. These objects can reference one another, but they are not interchangeable. When a ticket says “deploy the model,” orientation rewrites the request into resolvable questions: which artifact, for which workload, toward which purpose, under which conditions, owned by whom, and supported by what evidence?
The primary-treatment currentness ledger fixes what each citation can mean. `MLE-BSRC-045` and `MLE-BSRC-046` are unversioned, volatile employer postings verified 2026-08-18. The former is a single edge-computer-vision startup posting and the latter a single employer role, so neither is technical authority or a universal scope definition. For both: recheck before every freeze; replace if withdrawn, materially retitled, or no longer employer-controlled.

`MLE-BSRC-005` is final NIPS 2015, verified 2026-08-18. Its system-debt taxonomy is not a current product prescription or prevalence estimate. Recheck at blueprint, manuscript, and publication freeze for page and final-paper availability; pair contemporary mechanisms with current official documentation. `MLE-BSRC-007` is final IEEE Big Data 2017, verified 2026-08-18. Its 28-test method is not certification, a universal gate, current policy, or audited outcome. Recheck at publication freezes for the record and final paper; translate tests into workload evidence rather than copying a score threshold.

`MLE-BSRC-027` is the living AI RMF 1.0 Core rendering accessed and verified 2026-08-18. Its voluntary contextual outcomes are not a workload checklist, organization chart, certification, or legal determination. Recheck at every freeze and immediately if the revision notice, Govern, Map, Measure, or Manage outcomes, or a revised AI RMF is published. `MLE-BSRC-028` is final NIST AI 100-1, AI RMF 1.0, verified 2026-08-18. It is voluntary and under announced revision, not sector authorization or certification. Recheck at blueprint, manuscript, and publication freeze; retain 1.0 explicitly until a revision is actually published.

## MLE-CH-01-S03 — Interpret evidence without overclaiming

The strongest supportable conclusion from the entry artifacts is modest: Benchline can become oriented when identity, current evidence state, decision rights, and unresolved gaps are inspectable in one record. Orientation says nothing about model quality, dataset admissibility, safety, release readiness, or business value. That restraint is productive because later reviewers can tell exactly what has and has not been established.

A common counterexample is a versioned binary with excellent offline metrics. Teams often describe it as “ready” because it is reproducible enough to demo and the dashboard is green. Yet if no approved purpose or domain-validity owner is named, the evidence does not support even a candidate claim. Another counterexample is a sophisticated managed platform whose registry, deployment, and monitoring capabilities are excellent. Provider capability is evidence about a mechanism. It is not evidence that the particular workload is correctly scoped, evaluated, approved, or recoverable.

The authority map is also evidence, but only of routing. It proves that a decision has a named destination and that the MLE has not absorbed it. It does not prove the destination made a valid decision. A signature without decision scope, artifact identity, conditions, and limitations is weak evidence. Conversely, an unresolved external decision is not an engineering failure to conceal. It is a visible reason to HOLD.

`MLE-V01.1` is a semantic lifecycle rail paired with an authority matrix at this point in the chapter. Its essential labels are decision, evidence, state, owner, and next action. The long description reads the states in order, states that arrows are conditional rather than automatic, and identifies the labeled stop at each missing owner or evidence gap. Numeric and decision truth remains selectable text; color never carries meaning alone.

Evidence can mislead in three ways. It can be real but irrelevant, such as a tracker URL offered as product approval. It can be relevant but incomplete, such as a model card without a resolvable artifact. Or it can be current but volatile, such as a job posting used as timeless doctrine. The Bench Sheet requires the reviewer to identify which failure applies. That practice turns “we have evidence” into the sharper question “what decision does this evidence support, under what limits, and who may accept it?”

The evidence ceiling for `BL-00` is therefore explicit. It may establish workload orientation and owner routing. It may not establish population fitness, legal permission, platform certification, release approval, or production performance. Any prose or artifact that crosses that ceiling must be corrected before the record can pass.

The public-case transfer rules are decision tests, not optional commentary. For CASE-06, select tests from workload failure modes and consequences rather than a quota; replace point totals with evidence-bearing PASS, HOLD, or REJECT decisions and named owners; use current official mechanisms only as examples of a test category; and carry the method through all five ports without assuming a shared platform. For CASE-07, re-express every lesson as provider-neutral evidence fields and validation decisions, retain an as-of date beside each quantity or mechanism, demand the same lineage and comparison evidence across all ports, and never claim that a feature store or tracker reproduces Uber’s reported outcomes.

## MLE-CH-01-S04 — Worked trace and case truth

The worked trace uses three cases with different truth states. `CASE-01`, the Benchline Inspection Dossier, is a FICTIONAL SYNTHETIC CAPSTONE. Its request, owners, failures, and outcomes are constructed. It exists to make the orientation decision observable, not to imply anything about a real industrial inspection system. `CASE-06`, the Google ML Test Score production-readiness method, is a PUBLIC REPORTED CASE. `CASE-07`, Uber’s Michelangelo feature and deployment evidence pattern, is also a PUBLIC REPORTED CASE. Public cases provide bounded reported facts, not a license to reproduce their systems or outcomes.

For Benchline, the MLE reads `BL-ENTRY` and creates six records. The workload record names a synthetic edge inspection decision. The artifact inventory distinguishes the binary from run `run-019`. The evidence-state record says TRACKED RUN rather than CANDIDATE. The authority table names product, domain, evaluation, data, platform, security, safety, privacy, legal, release, operations, and business owners. The exclusions record states that no formal approval has occurred. The unresolved queue includes the missing domain-validity owner. Because that row is release-critical, the first execution returns HOLD rather than ORIENTED.

The positive trace adds a resolvable domain owner without altering any other input. A new input identity is computed, the owner row becomes complete, and `BL-00` is created with ORIENTED state. The earlier HOLD attempt remains in history. This separation matters: silently editing the first record would destroy the evidence of why the workload was blocked.

For `CASE-06`, the reported facts are that the 2017 paper presents 28 tests and monitoring needs spanning data and features, model development, infrastructure, and monitoring, and that the authors derived the rubric from their production experience. The attributed outcomes are the authors’ characterization of the rubric as a roadmap for readiness and technical-debt reduction. The allowed inference is that a model-only review can miss system evidence and that one release-critical missing test can justify HOLD. The forbidden inference is that a point total universally certifies production readiness. Its limitations include age, organization-specific experience, and the absence of a universal causal outcome. Transfer selects tests by failure and consequence, converts points into owner-bound dispositions, treats current official mechanisms only as category examples, and carries the decision across all five ports without assuming a shared platform.

For `CASE-07`, the reported facts are limited to Uber’s dated first-party accounts: the 2017 description of gaps and a six-step workflow, and the 2025 description of schema, imputation, statistics, model-report, and deployment-validation mechanisms. The reported adoption and scale numbers remain attributed outcomes, not independent audit results. The allowed inference is that stable feature, transformation, input, and run identities can make comparison gaps visible. The forbidden inference is that adopting a feature store recreates Uber’s results. Limitations keep the 2017 and 2025 system states separate and forbid reproduction of Uber’s prose, interface, branding, figures, or topology. Transfer restates lessons as provider-neutral evidence, dates every quantity and mechanism as-of its report, demands equivalent proof across all five ports, and never claims reproduced Uber outcomes.

The transfer lesson is not “copy Google” or “build Michelangelo.” It is to retain the decision job and evidence fields: identity, gap, owner, limitation, disposition, and next action. The constructed Benchline trace uses those fields without claiming a real production outcome. Public truth and synthetic truth meet only at a clearly labeled method transfer.

Now compare two possible write-ups of the same trace. The weak write-up says the model is production ready because it has been tracked, the organization has an ML platform, and an experienced MLE reviewed it. This sentence combines three real observations into one unsupported conclusion. Tracking says only that selected metadata exists. Platform availability says mechanisms exist. Reviewer experience says nothing about authority or missing evidence. The stronger write-up says the supplied binary and run are identifiable, the strongest evidenced state is TRACKED RUN, the owner map is incomplete, and orientation remains HOLD until domain validity and purpose are owned. That conclusion is narrower, but it is actionable and auditable.

## MLE-CH-01-S05 — Failure lab

The cases also show why limitations belong beside facts rather than at the end as legal decoration. The ML Test Score is useful precisely because it widens attention beyond the model, yet its point structure is not a universal qualification rule. Michelangelo is useful because its reports describe concrete platform mechanisms, yet those mechanisms and attributed quantities are organization-specific and date-bound. Benchline is useful because every field and failure can be controlled, yet none of its outcomes happened in reality. A reader who sees the limitation at the moment of transfer is less likely to mistake an illustrative mechanism for a result.

This treatment preserves source independence. The manuscript does not borrow source wording, diagrams, or implementation structure. It reconstructs the decision from accepted claims and asks what evidence would be required in any port. The result is book synthesis inside a declared ceiling: make workload evidence travel, make authority explicit, and refuse promotion when either is missing.

Both labs are `synthetic-deterministic`. They run offline against immutable fixtures, use a fixed clock and seed, and have no network, provider, production, credential, or authority mutation. `MLE-CH-01-LAB-01` is the positive path over fixture `FIX-MLE-CH-01-1`. It verifies the input identities, classifies the tracked run, resolves every owner, constructs the unresolved queue, and emits the deterministic `BL-00` record. Running the same bytes twice must produce the same canonical bytes and SHA-256.

`MLE-CH-01-LAB-02` uses fixture `FIX-MLE-CH-01-2` to exercise illegal promotion and self-approval. Its first mutation, OWNER-MISSING, deletes the domain-validity owner. The discriminating evidence is the empty authority route attached to a consequential decision, and the result is HOLD. The repair may add only a valid owner record and must create a new identity. Its second mutation, STATE-SKIP, relabels the tracked run as RELEASED. The validator compares prerequisites rather than trusting the label and returns REJECT. The repair returns to the strongest evidenced state; it does not manufacture intermediate records.

The third mutation, TITLE-SUBSTITUTION, inserts “Machine Learning Engineer” in place of the missing authority. This fails because a job title is not decision evidence. The result is HOLD with the actual domain owner named as next evidence. The diagnostic distinguishes the missing owner from the already-known absence of purpose approval. A good failure lab identifies the newly injected defect rather than producing a generic invalid result.

The laboratory dispositions are PASS, HOLD, REJECT, and REOPEN. PASS means the artifact satisfies this gate, not every later gate. HOLD means required evidence or ownership is missing but the current basis remains repairable. REJECT means the proposed transition or evidence is incompatible with the contract. REOPEN means changed upstream evidence invalidates a previously accepted basis. None of these dispositions automatically promotes the workload.

The lab record includes input hash, mutation identifier, expected and observed evidence, result, limitation, owner route, and next evidence. It also records prohibited effects. A test that changes an external account, sends a request, or grants authority would violate the companion boundary even if its assertion passed. The purpose is to demonstrate contract mechanics, not to simulate a production deployment.
## MLE-CH-01-S06 — Five-port transfer

The orientation invariant must survive all five ports. `PORT-MANAGED` may provide training, registry, serving, and observation as a service. The workload still needs exportable identities, configuration, limitations, evaluation, release, observation, and recovery evidence. An opaque provider state triggers HOLD; provider assurance never substitutes for qualification.

`PORT-CLASSICAL` may use local transformations and a statistical estimator. Simplicity does not erase the need for purpose, input identity, bounded run evidence, segment interpretation where relevant, package identity, operating envelope, and recovery. A feature-order or preprocessing mismatch is still a workload failure. Product, domain, evaluation, and data authority remain external.

`PORT-DEEP` adds parameterized training, an accelerator context, checkpoints, and a tensor-serving path. Scale increases the number of identities to preserve; it does not change the decision. The evidence names data, run, checkpoint, preprocessor, hardware context, evaluation, envelope, and recovery. Research and platform owners retain their decisions. A wrong checkpoint or preprocessing identity blocks the same orientation claim.

`PORT-EDGE` is Benchline’s native presentation: sensor or image input, constrained runtime, device package, and delayed feedback. The fictional setting allows precise contracts without a real industrial or safety claim. Hardware, operations, domain, safety, security, platform, and product authorities remain external. Sensor shift, resource pressure, or a stale package is a later failure pressure, not a reason to privilege edge engineering as the curriculum spine.

`PORT-SHARED` uses multi-tenant features, training, registry, serving, and observability. The dossier distinguishes tenant and workload identity from platform identity. Platform, SRE, and security own shared systems and fleet decisions. Tenant collision or shared-runtime change must be observable, but workload evidence cannot claim platform-wide assurance.

Across the ports, mechanics vary while the envelope does not. Each input names task, population, incumbent where available, artifact identities, execution context, authority map, perturbation, and expected evidence. Each result names disposition, state delta, limitation, owner route, and next evidence. No port is the reference implementation. A concept that makes sense only on one provider or model family has not yet been expressed as durable workload doctrine.

Port equality is tested with a translation exercise. Begin with the same missing-owner fixture. The managed adapter may expose provider job and registry identifiers; the classical adapter may expose a local pipeline and estimator; the deep adapter may expose a checkpoint and accelerator context; the edge adapter may expose device package and sensor input; the shared adapter may expose tenant and shared-service identifiers. Each must still return HOLD for the same missing authority, name the same consequential decision, and request the same owner evidence. A port that returns PASS because its platform offers an approval button has changed the meaning of the decision and fails transfer.

The reverse exercise begins with a complete owner map and changes a mechanism-specific field. An unavailable managed export, a classical feature-order mismatch, a deep checkpoint mismatch, a stale edge package, and a shared tenant collision are not identical failures. They invalidate different technical evidence. Yet the dossier shape remains stable: affected artifact, observed mismatch, strongest state, owner, limitation, disposition, and next action. This is why the five ports can teach one body of doctrine without pretending their implementations are alike.

Replaceability is especially important for currentness. Providers, libraries, device runtimes, and shared platforms change. If the chapter’s core claim depended on a particular API name, it would age with that API. Because the invariant is expressed as exportable identity and evidence, a later edition can replace a mechanism example without changing the decision center. The currentness note records the example’s identity and recheck trigger; the durable doctrine remains workload-specific.

The transfer test asks whether the dossier can move between ports without losing meaning. If a provider-specific run identifier cannot be exported, record the opacity. If a device cannot report immediately, record delayed observation. If a classical estimator lacks a checkpoint, use its immutable artifact identity rather than inventing deep-learning vocabulary. Equality means equivalent decisions and evidence shapes, not identical mechanisms.

## MLE-CH-01-S07 — Assessment and Qualification Gate

`MLE-CH-01-ASMT-01` asks the reader to repair an ambiguous intake and issue a complete `BL-00` or a justified HOLD. The supplied packet contains a binary, tracker link, partial purpose statement, and owner registry. The reader must annotate each field with its evidence source, classify the strongest state, build the authority table, and identify every unresolved decision. A response that merely recommends more testing does not pass because it leaves the decision and owner unclear.

### Qualification Gate

The gate passes only when workload identity, input identities, evidence state, decision, owner, limitation, authority route, and next evidence are explicit. The observable PASS evidence is a canonical `BL-00` whose state is ORIENTED and whose owner map contains every required decision. Missing or disputed authority produces HOLD. A forbidden promotion produces REJECT. A later change to purpose, population, runtime, or permitted use produces REOPEN to the appropriate earlier state.

The exact Answer intent is: Explain the decision, evidence, state, owner, and next action. A strong answer distinguishes three near misses. A complete model inventory without purpose remains HOLD. A complete authority map paired with a fabricated RELEASED label is REJECT. A previously oriented workload whose intended use changes is REOPEN, because the old orientation no longer supports the changed task. The answer explains why the evidence supports the disposition and what it does not support.

The MLE ceiling is part of the rubric. The reader may identify owners, prepare evidence, and issue the workload orientation disposition. The reader may not approve the product purpose, validate the domain, accept safety or legal risk, certify the platform, or declare business acceptance. An otherwise complete submission that crosses that line fails the authority portion of the gate.

Retry is evidence bearing. The reader creates a new artifact version, cites the failed field, supplies only the new evidence, and preserves the previous attempt. Silent correction earns no credit because it prevents a reviewer from reconstructing why the state changed. This discipline begins in orientation and persists through retirement.

The scoring rubric rewards restraint as well as completeness. A reader who marks every row PASS without evidence fails even if the proposed owners sound plausible. A reader who marks HOLD, identifies the one missing consequential owner, and explains the supported state can pass. Engineering judgment is not optimism; it is the ability to name what the record supports, what it cannot support, and the least additional evidence needed to move responsibly.

Reviewers should also test internal consistency. The state label must agree with prerequisites. The decision rationale must cite the input identities actually hashed. The owner route must correspond to the missing or disputed decision. The next evidence must be capable of resolving the stated gap. If the record says the domain owner is missing but requests another offline metric, it has confused a technical measurement with authority. If it says purpose changed but retains the previous contract unchanged, it has hidden a reopen trigger.

## MLE-CH-01-S08 — Durable handoff

Durable dossier record: BL-00 version 1.0.0 consumes BL-ENTRY from incoming state UNORIENTED without silent repair. Its frozen planning linkage binds the accepted input identity to hash aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa; that accepted linkage is not presented as a newly executed hash. The record carries workload orientation, artifact inventory, strongest evidenced state, unresolved decisions, owners, authority routes, limitations, and next evidence. Its evidence manifest is MLE-BCLM-001, MLE-BCLM-002, MLE-BCLM-003. Earlier dossier bytes remain immutable, and any correction creates a new artifact identity and lineage edge.

The only legal outgoing state is ORIENTED. HOLD to RELEASABLE and REJECT to RELEASABLE are forbidden transitions. The four reopen routes are exact: purpose or intended use goes to CONTRACTED; data, labels, features, or population goes to ADMISSIBLE; runtime, dependencies, interface, or serving envelope goes to RECONSTRUCTIBLE; authority, constraint, or permitted use goes to CONTRACTED. A reopen invalidates only affected downstream evidence, retains the triggering record, names its authority owner, and cannot silently promote state.

The chapter-specific rechecks travel with the record: Start with tests of the supplied artifact and the decision it can support; confine live job postings to a clearly date-stamped market-evidence inset. Require a lifecycle display that places MODEL FILE, TRACKED RUN, CANDIDATE, QUALIFIED, RELEASED, and OPERATING side by side while keeping every advance conditional on evidence. Make the authority map executable as an owner-and-decision table with a fail-closed missing-owner state. These are evidence obligations, not claims that an illustration, lab, or external system has run. The limitations also travel: the dossier proves only this chapter’s bounded decision under its named inputs and fixtures; PASS cannot certify later gates, public-case outcomes remain attributed, and synthetic evidence remains synthetic-deterministic.

Prohibited claims remain explicit: No role-title taxonomy, organization design, product authorization, formal approval, or platform certification. Do not invent public-case facts, outcomes, authority, production results, or certification. Do not activate Phase 08, create code, or generate assets from this blueprint. The continuity rule is: Consume BL-ENTRY without silent repair; produce BL-00 for MLE-CH-02. Chapter 2 may consume the orientation charter only to build the purpose contract; it must not infer product approval or repair a missing owner. This exact next dependency, together with the evidence manifest, legal state, limitations, rechecks, and lineage, is the handoff; no asset, external approval, or production result is implied.
