Candidate comparison stresses each adapter differently. A managed platform may conceal eliminated trials behind its tuning service. Nothing in the exercise demonstrates real accuracy, latency, safety, or economic value. It demonstrates only that the selection contract survives a change of implementation.

Search-service exports, experiment-tracker schemas, estimator defaults, accelerator software, device constraints, and shared-resource policies can all change after a nomination. The enduring rule is to preserve the full contest and its limitations; the current mechanism must be dated and rechecked. A tool upgrade does not retroactively invalidate the historical score, but it may make the old basis inappropriate for a new comparison. In that case the ledger reopens and adds evidence under a new identity. This prevents a living API or default setting from becoming an unstated part of the selection doctrine and keeps the durable decision method separate from today's implementation.
