# MLE-CH-02 — Contract the Task and Decision Rights

Blueprint status: Phase 07 production specification; not manuscript prose. Author: Komal Nakrani. Creative system: Learning Systems Test Bench.

## Frozen identity and production target

- Chapter/order: `MLE-CH-02` / 2
- Slug/part: `contract-the-task-and-decision-rights` / `PART-01`
- Decision job: Convert an approved purpose into a bounded task contract with explicit decision owners and a NO-ML or HOLD path.
- Thesis: A model cannot be qualified until intended use, excluded use, population, decision, constraints, and authority are reviewable.
- Reader endpoint: Issue a versioned task contract whose missing owner or unjustified ML use fails closed.
- Milestone: `BL-01` — Task and authority contract
- State: `ORIENTED` → `CONTRACTED`
- Word range: 4,300-4,900 words
- Word-range rationale: The task-contract airlock needs room for intended and excluded use, population, consequences, owners, and a readable NO-ML branch.

## Observable objectives

- Convert an approved purpose into machine-checkable intended-use, excluded-use, population, context, consequence, assumption, limitation, and evidence fields.
- Fail a contract with a missing owner, disputed purpose, or changed population and name the next authority.
- Select NO-ML when a deterministic rule satisfies the accepted contract.
- Demonstrate identical contract semantics across all five ports.

## Prerequisites and five-sentence dossier bridge

Prerequisite artifacts: `BL-00`.

1. The input is BL-00, including the workload identity, evidence state, exclusions, owner registry, and unresolved-decision queue.
2. The prior state is ORIENTED, which permits task contraction but does not imply that ML is justified or authorized.
3. This chapter adds intended and excluded uses, population, operating context, consequences, constraints, evidence needs, incumbent, decision owners, and exit semantics.
4. BL-01 moves the dossier to CONTRACTED only when the purpose is evaluable and every required authority is named; otherwise it records HOLD or NO-ML.
5. Chapter 03 consumes BL-01 and cannot choose thresholds or consequences that product and domain owners did not accept.

## Owned decision and retained authority

- MLE responsibility: Translate an approved purpose into a versioned technical task-and-authority contract with explicit exit paths.
- Authority owner: Product owns purpose and priority; domain and formal authorities own validity and permissibility.
- MLE ceiling: The MLE translates an approved purpose into technical evidence requirements but cannot authorize it.
- Boundaries: `BND-13`, `BND-15`, `BND-16`, `BND-17`
- Escalation: Route disputed purpose to product, validity to domain owners, permissibility to formal authorities, and missing decision rights to the accountable organization owner.
- Explicit non-scope: No product prioritization, domain sign-off, risk acceptance, legal interpretation, or declaration that ML must be used.

## Skill procedure and evidence interpretation

1. Bind intended use and excluded use to a versioned workload identity.
2. Name population, operating context, decision, consequences, constraints, and assumptions.
3. Attach evidence requirements and accepting owners to every consequential field.
4. Execute the incumbent or deterministic alternative before assuming ML.
5. Issue CONTRACTED, HOLD, REJECT, or NO-ML with owner and next evidence.

- Strongest supportable conclusion: The task airlock opens only for an evaluable purpose with explicit exclusions, consequences, owners, and evidence needs.
- Counterexample: A technically feasible classifier remains on HOLD when no authority owns the changed population or false-negative consequence.
- Limitation: A complete contract makes assumptions reviewable but cannot make the purpose valid or permissible.

## Failure injections and diagnostics

- PURPOSE-DISPUTED: conflict the requested action with the approved purpose and require HOLD.
- POPULATION-CHANGED: introduce an uncontracted surface type and require REOPEN to CONTRACTED.
- OWNER-MISSING: remove consequence acceptance and require HOLD with a named route.

Diagnostic evidence must distinguish the newly injected failure from pre-existing gaps. Repair may change only the failed contract field and must create a new artifact identity. HOLD and REJECT can never promote directly to RELEASABLE, and the workload owner cannot self-approve an external gate.
