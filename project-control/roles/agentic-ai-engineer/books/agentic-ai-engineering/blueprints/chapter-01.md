## Observable objectives

The reader can distinguish model, agent harness, product, platform, domain, and
human-authority layers; diagnose whether control flow is predetermined or
model-directed; assign primary ownership for failure classes; reject title-based
scope inflation; and produce an auditable responsibility charter.

## Prerequisites and five-sentence dossier bridge

Prerequisites: production software ownership, basic LLM/tool literacy, incident
reasoning, and willingness to separate technical evidence from organizational
authority.

1. The reader enters with no FieldOps dossier and a vague request to “build an agent.”
2. The organization has conflated the model, workflow, product outcome, platform, and safety decision.
3. Chapter 01 creates the first stable system boundary before any autonomy is approved.
4. Every later `AR-*` artifact will inherit the owners and exclusions recorded here.
5. Chapter 02 receives `AR-01 v0.1.0` and tests whether an agent is justified at all.

## Owned concepts and explicit non-scope

Own: operational agent/workflow definition; harness responsibility; action and
effect accountability; evidence obligations; escalation and boundary records.
Do not teach model training/inference, broad AI product discovery, enterprise
platform design, customer adoption, domain risk acceptance, legal liability, or
formal security/safety approval. Those remain with LLM/ML, Applied AI, platform,
FDE/product, domain, legal, security, and safety owners.

## Skill procedure and implementation content

1. Draw the system boundary from user request to external effect.
2. Identify which component selects each transition.
3. Name the artifact and observable evidence for every decision.
4. Assign exactly one primary accountable owner; add collaborators separately.
5. Mark authority that cannot be delegated by the harness.
6. Add escalation triggers and unresolved questions.
7. Re-run the map for model regression, tool outage, wrong product goal,
   injection, unsafe domain advice, and platform failure.

Architecture content is a responsibility-layer diagram and a typed charter
schema, not executable code. Current examples: OpenAI and Anthropic definitions
may be compared as dated terminology only. Durable principle: ownership follows
the actual decision and enforcement point. Volatile material: provider product
names, job titles, framework definitions, and NIST revision status.

## Scenario, failures, mistakes, and trade-offs

FieldOps leadership asks one engineer to own business results, infrastructure,
safety, and “the AI.” Failure injection: a prompt-injected manual leads to a
wrong reservation while the inventory service is degraded. Diagnosis must split
content trust, harness/tool enforcement, product requirement, platform health,
and formal risk authority.

Common mistakes: calling every LLM feature an agent; assigning the model agency
without examining the harness; using a team chart as technical evidence;
absorbing specialist authority; and leaving shared ownership with no primary
owner. Trade-off: a narrower boundary increases coordination cost but preserves
accountability and prevents one role from making unsupported decisions.

## Exercises and assessment

- Exercise: classify five failures plus seven change decisions. Output:
  `AR-01/responsibility-charter.yaml` and boundary memo. Expected failure:
  duplicate primary owners or an unowned effect. Adjacent-role limit: no student
  decides legal/safety acceptability.
- Assessment: 20-point rubric—layer accuracy 5, one-owner discipline 5,
  evidence/escalation 5, explicit exclusions 5. Pass requires no effect or
  authority decision without an owner.
- Acceptance evidence: every decision ID maps to owner, artifact, evidence,
  collaborator, escalation, and non-scope.
