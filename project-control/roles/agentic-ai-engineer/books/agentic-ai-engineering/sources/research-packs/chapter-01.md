# Chapter 01 Research Pack — The Profession Behind the Agent

## Frozen job and evidence question

Define the profession by accountability for model-directed action, then separate
the agent harness from the model, product, platform, domain, security/safety,
and human-authority layers. The chapter must answer: when a system fails, which
decision belongs to the Agentic AI Engineer and which must be handed to another
owner? It produces `AR-01` responsibility charter and boundary memo.

## Claims to carry

- `AGE-BCLM-001` (normative synthesis): the role owns the deterministic action
  loop and its evidence without absorbing adjacent authorities.
- `AGE-BCLM-002` (supported definition): distinguish workflows with predefined
  control flow from systems where a model materially directs tool use.

The definition is operational, not a claim that the industry uses one title.
The manuscript must not turn employer role evidence from Phase 01 into technical
best practice.

## Source findings

- `AGE-BSRC-001` and `AGE-BSRC-002` both distinguish workflows and agents by
  control-flow ownership and tool-directed autonomy. They support terminology,
  not a complete profession boundary.
- `AGE-BSRC-036` requires context, accountability, measurement, and lifecycle
  risk management. It supports named owners but does not allocate this specific
  role.
- Architecture boundary remains authoritative: Applied AI owns mechanism and
  product choice; LLM/ML roles own model depth; platform/SRE own shared runtime;
  security and safety retain formal review; product/domain owners retain outcome
  and real-world authority.

## Case and FieldOps Relay research use

Use `AGE-CASE-012` only. In the kickoff memo, leadership calls every LLM feature
an agent and asks one engineer to own business results, platform uptime and
safety. The research task is to classify 12 decisions into owned, partnered,
or escalated. The deterministic scheduler handling 92% is a fictional failure
injection, never a measured company fact.

Required `AR-01` fields: system boundary, model boundary, action/effect boundary,
authority boundary, named accountable owner, collaborators, evidence produced,
explicit exclusions, escalation trigger, and unresolved decision.

## Contradictions and limitations

- Industry sources use “agent,” “workflow,” “assistant,” and “agentic system”
  inconsistently. Resolve with inspectable control flow, not brand vocabulary.
- Model capability does not allocate professional responsibility.
- NIST AI RMF is voluntary and cross-sector; it is not an Agentic AI Engineer
  competency standard.
- Legal liability, regulated authorization and organizational RACI are outside
  the chapter's authority.

## Phase 07 blueprint seed

Open with a responsibility incident, not a history of agents. Sequence:
operational definition -> five-layer responsibility map -> failure-classification
method -> FieldOps charter workshop -> boundary test -> dossier update. Minimum
exercise: classify a prompt-injection-caused reservation, dependency outage,
wrong product requirement, unsafe domain advice, and model regression. The
chapter passes only if every item has exactly one primary owner and no specialist
authority is silently assigned to the Agentic AI Engineer. Visual anchor:
`F01.1`; `F01.2` remains optional and must use identity-preserving ImageGen only.

## Gaps to keep visible

There is no universal professional standard for this exact title. Present the
boundary as the Komal catalog's evidence-based contract, invite organizations to
map it to their own accountabilities, and do not claim legal completeness.
