# Chapter 01 Blueprint — The Deployed Outcome

## Purpose and exit capability

Establish the Komal FDE operating model. The reader can classify work as FDE core, shared, supporting, or specialist-owned; define a production outcome; and write a responsibility charter that separates outcome leadership from formal authority.

## Prerequisites and non-scope

- Prerequisite: production delivery experience described in the book architecture.
- Non-scope: a universal employer definition, sales/account ownership, legal or security authorization, heroic individual ownership of institutional risk.

## Concepts, skills, and decision models

- Deployment versus release; workflow versus interface; output versus outcome; adoption, stabilization, handoff, and product leverage.
- Deployed-outcome lifecycle: discover, contract, scope, design, build, verify, launch, stabilize, transfer, learn—with evidence-driven returns.
- Responsibility classifier: consequence, authority, evidence, execution, and escalation.
- Skill: write “done” as sustained workflow value inside named guardrails and ownership boundaries.

## Architecture, implementation, and tools

- Architecture content: lifecycle and responsibility boundaries only; structural design begins in Chapter 6.
- Current examples: OpenAI and Palantir role materials (`volatile/employer-specific`) illustrate observed practice. A plain Markdown decision log (`durable format`) records the charter.

## Failures, mistakes, and tradeoffs

- Shipping equals done; role title equals authority; “customer success” without evidence; permanent embedded dependency; universalizing one job posting.
- Tradeoff: broad outcome ownership increases continuity but also overreach/burnout risk unless authority and exit are explicit.

## Exercise and completion evidence

Classify twelve mixed tasks, defend two disputed boundaries, and write the charter. Pass when every task names owner/authority/evidence/escalation and “done” contains outcome, guardrail, duration, and handoff state.
