# Chapter 04 Blueprint — Contract for an Outcome

## Purpose and exit capability

The reader can translate workflow evidence into outcome, leading, adoption, system, and guardrail measures plus baselines, decision rights, non-goals, and exit/stop criteria that reject an impressive but useless prototype.

## Prerequisites and non-scope

- Prerequisite: verified `OA-02` workflow and evidence gaps.
- Non-scope: commercial/legal contract drafting, invented targets, guaranteed causality, or treating use/satisfaction/accuracy as outcome.

## Concepts, skills, and decision models

- Outcome contract anatomy; metric tree; gaming test; cohort/segment; baseline and observation window; evidence owner.
- Decision-rights actions: recommend, approve/accept, execute, inform, escalate.
- Attribution ladder: temporal association, comparison, plausible contribution, causal design; state the available level.
- Skill: test whether a metric can improve while user or guardrail outcomes worsen.

## Architecture, implementation, and tools

- Define measurement events and data feasibility without prematurely choosing the full telemetry stack.
- Current tools: analytics/experimentation platforms (`volatile/vendor-specific`) may calculate measures; canonical definitions and decision rules remain versioned Markdown/JSON (`durable`).

## Scenario and artifacts

- Build `OA-03` outcome contract, metric tree, baselines-needed register, adoption measures, safety/privacy/cost guardrails, non-goals, assumptions, decision-rights map, and pilot/stabilization exit criteria.
- Expose the gaming route that improves closure time by excluding difficult cases.

## Cases and bounded use

## Failures, mistakes, and tradeoffs

- Vanity metrics, averages hiding cohorts, post-hoc target changes, unmeasurable guardrails, ownerless approval.
- Tradeoff: measurement burden versus decision value; retain measures that can change a decision and log gaps explicitly.

## Exercise and completion evidence

Audit a flawed metric tree and write a go/stop decision rule. Pass when each metric has definition, source, segment, cadence, owner, limitation, and associated decision.
