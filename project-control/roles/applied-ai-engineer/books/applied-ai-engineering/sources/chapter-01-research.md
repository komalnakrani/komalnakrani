# Chapter 01 Research — Behavior, Not Magic

## Architecture anchors

- Primary domains: `AAE-K01`, `AAE-K02`, `AAE-K11`

- Forecast figures: `F01.1`, `F01.2`

## Research question

What makes complete AI-powered product behavior—not a model, prompt, score, or demo—the Applied AI Engineer's durable unit of accountability?

## Claim and evidence map

- **C01.1 — AI risk and quality belong to a system lifecycle, not a model artifact alone.** `AAE-S001` describes lifecycle risk management; `AAE-S005` and `AAE-S006` document cross-component engineering and entanglement. The book's role conclusion is a Komal synthesis, not language attributed to NIST or those studies.
- **C01.2 — Production readiness requires evidence across data, model, code, infrastructure, and monitoring.** `AAE-S007` supplies a multi-layer test rubric. Treat the rubric as question generation, not a universal score.
- **C01.3 — Documentation can organize claims, owners, and limitations but does not verify them.** `AAE-S055` motivates system factsheets; `AAE-S001` preserves governance and risk-role context.
- **C01.4 — The applied engineer's distinctive decision is whether the combined system behaves usefully and dependably for a defined task.** Phase 01 role evidence supplies employer support; `AAE-S005` and `AAE-S006` support why ordinary software/model boundaries are insufficient.
- **C01.5 — Formal risk acceptance and specialist authority do not arise from implementation ownership.** `AAE-S001` and `AAE-S054` distinguish governance and contextual role assignment. The chapter must state authority rather than imply it.

## Durable synthesis

Use four nested objects: capability, component, product behavior, and authorized outcome. The Applied AI Engineer owns technical behavior evidence at the product/system layer. They collaborate upward and sideways on value, domain truth, policy, platform, and risk authority.

## Cases

- `AAE-C010` shows why model performance is only one production-readiness surface.
- `AAE-C012` begins with a Patchwork request for an “AI finder” and forces classification of model, application, product, domain, and authority decisions.

## Disputes and limits

Team boundaries vary. A small company may assign product, ML platform, or customer-delivery work to the same person. Classification follows the decision and accountability, not the title. “End to end” must never mean unlimited access or institutional authority.

## Remaining gaps

No release-blocking research gap. Phase 07 must connect each boundary classification to an observable scenario rather than a vocabulary quiz.

## Blueprint constraints

Open with a behavior regression that cannot be repaired by “better prompting” alone. End with a responsibility charter and evidence-loop map. This remains research architecture, not finished manuscript prose.

## Manuscript prohibitions

Do not anthropomorphize “the AI,” present model capability as product readiness, or describe the engineer as a universal owner of risk, value, infrastructure, or domain truth.
