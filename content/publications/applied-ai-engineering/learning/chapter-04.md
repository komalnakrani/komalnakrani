# Learning Pack 04 - Choose the Simplest Adequate Mechanism

> NOT FOR LIVE CERTIFICATION BANK. These formative prompts are publication learning material only.

## Recall and explain

1. Why is the mechanism ladder not a maturity ladder?
2. Distinguish lexical retrieval, multimodal retrieval, ranking, generation, and action.
3. Name the twelve dimensions of the mechanism tradeoff surface.
4. Why can a stronger benchmark model be a worse product mechanism?
5. What six gates must adaptive tool use pass?

## Scenario decisions

### One-call architecture

A team sends the user query and full catalog to a generative model and asks for a candidate, explanation, and seller message. Decompose the behavior into stable candidate, decision, presentation, and effect interfaces. Identify which decisions should be deterministic.

### More recall, worse consequence

Hybrid retrieval finds more relevant listings but introduces unresolved-unit candidates into the top position. Explain why one aggregate score cannot settle the decision. Propose a bounded correction or rejection.

### Function calling

A model emits a valid `send_message` call. Apply the task-value, authority, evidence, effect-control, evaluation, and operating gates. Do not confuse structural validity with authorization.

## Applied exercise

Score six portfolios for one behavior contract: deterministic, predictive, lexical/structured retrieval, multimodal retrieval, generative, and tool/agent. For each, record contract coverage, data, consequential error, calibration, latency tail, cost, privacy, attack surface, ownership, change, fallback, and authority. Reject at least two and choose the cheapest discriminating experiment.

## Optional formative MCQs

1. Which claim does a strong multimodal benchmark support?
   - A. Patchwork should ship it
   - B. The mechanism is a feasible option deserving local evidence
   - C. Similarity proves compatibility
   - D. Generation is required
   - **Answer: B.**

2. What may override a known deterministic incompatibility?
   - A. A higher rank score
   - B. A generated explanation
   - C. Nothing unless the authorized domain rule itself changes
   - D. A user click
   - **Answer: C.**

3. When is agentic action justified?
   - A. Whenever a model supports tools
   - B. When bounded action adds task value beyond simpler confirmed workflows and all six gates pass
   - C. When the demo is impressive
   - D. When the task has multiple steps
   - **Answer: B.**

## Advanced challenge

Design stable interfaces that let lexical, vector-like, and hybrid retrievers share a candidate contract while a deterministic decision layer dominates permissions and exclusions. List the component and end-to-end claims your local synthetic tests can and cannot prove.

## Completion evidence

Pass when the recommendation is challengeable, external evidence retains context limits, generation can fail its separate gate, and prohibited tools have no implementation path.
