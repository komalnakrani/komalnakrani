# Learning Pack 11 - Combine Machine, Human, and Domain Judgment

> NOT FOR LIVE CERTIFICATION BANK. These formative prompts are publication learning material only.

## Recall and explain

1. Describe the judgment stack from invariant through authority.
2. Why does agreement not prove correctness?
3. What belongs in a model-grader allowed-use statement?
4. Why must evaluator-development cases remain separate?
5. What makes `cannot judge` useful evidence?
6. When should an evaluator be rejected rather than automated?

## Scenario decisions

### Verbose wrong winner

A grader prefers a long guaranteed-fit answer over concise evidence. Design position/verbosity probes, disposition, fallback, and fresh calibration.

### Specialist dispute

Two specialists disagree under incomplete evidence. Classify the disagreement and choose product state, adjudication path, and authority.

### User preference versus authority

Users prefer certainty. Explain which user evidence is valid and why it cannot authorize unsupported behavior.

## Applied exercise

Assign twelve system claims to deterministic checks, references, bounded model graders, trained raters, task users, specialists, or authorities. Run a blind sample and leave one dispute unresolved.

## Optional formative MCQs

1. Who judges schema conformance? A deterministic validator.
2. Does human majority establish truth? No.
3. What happens when a grader misses a critical stop condition? Reject or narrow its use.

## Advanced challenge

Design an evaluator router and prove it does not route critical segments to weaker evidence.

## Completion evidence

Pass when each claim has a credible bounded evaluator, calibration evidence, failure fallback, unresolved route, and authority.
