# Learning Pack 01 - Behavior, Not Magic

> NOT FOR LIVE CERTIFICATION BANK. These formative prompts are publication learning material only.

## Recall and explain

1. Define capability, component, product behavior, and authorized outcome using one consistent example.
2. Why can a model pass its evaluation while the product violates its user promise?
3. What is the difference between owning technical behavior evidence and accepting institutional risk?
4. Explain why a demonstration is useful evidence but not a production-readiness decision.
5. Name the seven movements of the applied AI evidence loop and describe one legitimate return path.

## Scenario decisions

### A polished regression

A support summarizer returns valid JSON and passes a provider evaluation. The interface labels every summary "verified," although the source ticket can be stale and no reviewer queue exists. Classify the failure across capability, component, behavior, and outcome. Identify the smallest safe product state.

### End-to-end ownership

An engineer is told to "own this end to end" and discovers that release requires a new private-data purpose, a domain definition, and a security exception. Write a response that drives closure without claiming any of those authorities.

### A readiness score

A team averages strong model, latency, and deployment results with a failing critical-segment control and calls the feature 82 percent ready. Explain why the aggregation is misleading. Produce a decision packet instead.

## Applied exercise

Choose an AI-powered behavior you know. Create a responsibility charter with:

- bounded task behavior;
- technical evidence you will own;
- dependencies you will diagnose but not permanently own;
- explicit non-scope;
- required product, domain, platform, privacy, security, and risk decisions;
- escalation triggers;
- one failure trace across task, data/context, mechanism, application, interface, operation, and authority.

Then classify twelve decisions using decision, artifact, evidence, consequence, working owner, authority, and escalation. At least three decisions must stay outside the implementation owner's formal authority.

## Optional formative MCQs

1. A ranker improves NDCG while users receive unsupported compatibility badges. Which object passed and which failed?
   - A. Outcome passed; component failed
   - B. Component evidence improved; product behavior failed
   - C. Both passed because the metric improved
   - D. Neither can be discussed without revenue
   - **Answer: B.**

2. What does a system factsheet prove by itself?
   - A. Every documented claim is verified
   - B. Formal risk is accepted
   - C. Claims and limitations are organized for inspection
   - D. The feature is ready
   - **Answer: C.**

3. Who authorizes residual production risk?
   - A. Whoever wrote the prompt
   - B. Whoever owns the evidence repository
   - C. The role assigned the relevant decision right
   - D. The most senior engineer present
   - **Answer: C.**

## Advanced challenge

Write a one-page argument against both extremes: "the model team owns only the endpoint" and "the applied engineer owns everything." Your alternative must preserve continuous technical accountability, component ownership, and named institutional authority.

## Completion evidence

Pass when another reader can use your charter to identify what is being built, what evidence remains missing, who must decide, and why a locally successful model result is insufficient.
