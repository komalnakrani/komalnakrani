# Learning Pack 10 - Name the Errors That Matter

> NOT FOR LIVE CERTIFICATION BANK. These formative prompts are publication learning material only.

## Recall and explain

1. Distinguish error, cause, and consequence.
2. Why are calibration and correctness different?
3. What makes correct abstention a successful outcome?
4. Why can a threshold sweep fail to reveal an acceptable operating point?
5. How can an average hide a critical segment?
6. What belongs in a release disposition beyond metrics?

## Scenario decisions

### Aggregate winner, critical loser

The challenger improves recall but presents one incompatible and one denied-permission case. Produce the taxonomy records, segment report, gate, correction experiment, and authority handoff.

### Calibrated but unusable

A score is calibrated globally but the long-tail segment has weak evidence and high abstention. Decide what may be shown to users and what additional evidence is required.

### Proxy reward

Confident explanations increase clicks. Evidence fidelity declines. Separate relevance, comprehension, permission, long-term task value, and business signals.

## Applied exercise

Evaluate two configurations. Predeclare taxonomy, severity, detectability, reversibility, threshold, critical segments, and guardrails. Report raw counts, aggregate/segment/intersection views, abstention, reliability data, and a disposition.

## Optional formative MCQs

1. What does calibration establish? A frequency relationship under defined labels/population, not individual correctness or authority.
2. Can recall gain compensate for permission exposure? No; a declared zero-tolerance control blocks it.
3. Is challenge-set failure rate prevalence? No.

## Advanced challenge

Design a criterion record and threshold policy for a non-generative forecast, including segment consequence and correct abstention.

## Completion evidence

Pass when the aggregate winner can be rejected by preserved consequential evidence and every threshold/guardrail names scope and authority.
