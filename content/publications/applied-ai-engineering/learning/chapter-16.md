# Learning Pack 16 - Implement Controls and Preserve Authority

> NOT FOR LIVE CERTIFICATION BANK. These formative prompts are publication learning material only.

## Recall and explain

1. Distinguish policy, configured mechanism, tested control, and accepted residual.
2. What belongs in a control evidence chain?
3. Why is a prompt not a security boundary?
4. Why must semantic and effect controls follow schema validation?
5. Which states belong in a bounded tool sequence?
6. Why can a qualified reviewer still lack release authority?

## Scenario decisions

### Seller manipulation

Defeat a prompt-only guardrail and design allowed-field isolation, validation, monitor, residual, reviewer, and authority.

### Ambiguous legacy compatibility

Choose strengthen, reduce scope, delay, or stop when deterministic coverage is absent and a deadline remains.

### Unknown message outcome

Trace exact confirmation, idempotency, audit, reconciliation, user-visible state, and recovery without repeating the effect.

## Applied exercise

Complete six control chains with implementation, version, adversarial evidence, negative assertion, monitor, owner, residual, reviewer, and named authority.

## Optional formative MCQs

1. Does a policy sentence prove enforcement? No.
2. Does user confirmation grant system permission? No.
3. Can an engineer accept formal residual risk by implementing the control? No.

## Advanced challenge

Disable one supplementary provider safeguard and prove application permission, compatibility, and agency invariants still hold.

## Completion evidence

Pass when prompt-only assurance loses, six controls are executable, every effect is bounded, and one residual remains with its proper authority.
