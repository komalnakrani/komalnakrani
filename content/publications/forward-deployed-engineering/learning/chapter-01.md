# Chapter 01 Learning Pack - The Deployed Outcome

> NOT FOR LIVE CERTIFICATION BANK

These materials reinforce the chapter and may be revised freely. They must never be copied directly into a live certification bank.

## Conceptual questions

1. Why can a technically successful release coexist with a failed deployment?
2. Explain each element of the deployed-outcome definition: measurable, sustained, workflow value, live environment, guardrails, used, supportable, and owned.
3. Distinguish release evidence, deployment evidence, and production-outcome evidence.
4. Why does outcome leadership require explicit authority boundaries rather than fewer boundaries?
5. What makes the lifecycle a loop instead of a waterfall?
6. Distinguish FDE-core, shared, supporting, and specialist-owned work without assigning those labels permanently to job titles.
7. Why is adoption evidence necessary but insufficient for an outcome claim?
8. What evidence would justify treating a repeated customer pattern as a product-leverage candidate?

## Scenario questions

### Scenario A - unrestricted export

A sponsor offers an unrestricted export of three years of production tickets to accelerate discovery. The data includes customer names, locations, technician notes, and attachments. The FDE can access it immediately.

- Which part of the response is FDE core?
- Which decisions require customer authority?
- Propose two minimized evidence paths.
- State the exact condition under which discovery should pause.

### Scenario B - automatic action

Leaders want a model recommendation to place a parts order automatically. The integration is technically straightforward, but the organization has not defined acceptable incorrect-order consequences or approval authority.

- Classify the work and decisions.
- Propose a reversible first path.
- What evidence could change the non-scope decision later?

### Scenario C - low use

Only 20 percent of an enabled cohort uses a new workflow. The sponsor asks for mandatory training.

- Give at least six competing explanations for low use.
- Which evidence would distinguish them?
- Which responses belong to the FDE, product/design, operations, managers, and support?

### Scenario D - deadline exception

The identity dependency is late. A shared long-lived credential would preserve the launch date.

- Write the decision options and consequences.
- Name the evidence and owner required for any temporary exception.
- State when delay or reduced scope is the correct recommendation.

## Applied exercise - responsibility charter

Choose a real or fictional workflow. Produce a two-page charter containing:

- bounded engagement purpose;
- provisional production-outcome hypothesis;
- FDE-core, shared, supporting, and specialist-owned responsibilities;
- recommend/approve/execute/inform distinctions for five material decisions;
- evidence obligations;
- escalation triggers;
- exit conditions;
- explicit non-scope.

Review the charter using these tests:

1. Can every requested access item be challenged later without invalidating the charter?
2. Does the charter name a decision the FDE must drive but may not approve?
3. Can the engagement stop for lack of evidence or authority?
4. Does “done” require user and operator capability rather than document delivery?
5. Does the charter avoid promising a preferred solution before discovery?

## Optional practice MCQs

### 1. Which statement best represents production-outcome evidence?

A. Version 1.0 was deployed with all automated checks green.  
B. The target cohort used the feature 400 times.  
C. The bounded cohort showed the contracted workflow direction over the agreed window, guardrails and operating evidence remained within accepted bounds, and limitations were recorded.  
D. The executive sponsor approved the demonstration.

Answer: **C**. A, B, and D may be evidence inputs but do not establish the complete bounded outcome.

### 2. An FDE discovers that a safety approval has no named customer owner. What is the best next action?

A. Approve the path because the FDE owns the outcome.  
B. Ask a developer to accept the risk in the issue tracker.  
C. Make the missing owner, evidence, consequence, and deadline explicit; escalate and block or reduce the path until legitimate authority exists.  
D. Remove the safety requirement from scope without recording it.

Answer: **C**. Outcome leadership keeps the decision moving while preserving formal authority.

### 3. Which item is most likely supporting rather than FDE-core?

A. Defining a bounded outcome and guardrails.  
B. Making interface failure semantics explicit.  
C. Producing a decorative demonstration video that does not resolve an evidence or adoption blocker.  
D. Preparing a rollout and rollback decision record.

Answer: **C**. A demonstration can become material, but decoration alone does not maintain the outcome chain.

## Advanced challenge - claim maturity audit

Take a project statement such as “the pilot was successful” and rewrite it at all six claim-maturity stages from the chapter:

1. request;
2. discovery hypothesis;
3. bounded outcome contract;
4. readiness claim;
5. production outcome claim;
6. ownership and leverage claim.

For each stage, list the evidence available, the evidence still missing, the authorized decision owner, and the prohibited overclaim. The challenge passes only if later claims do not silently inherit evidence that was never produced.
