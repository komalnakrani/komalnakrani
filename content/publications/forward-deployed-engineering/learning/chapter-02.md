# Chapter 02 Learning Pack - Enter the Customer System

> NOT FOR LIVE CERTIFICATION BANK

## Conceptual questions

1. Why must a discovery question precede an access request?
2. Distinguish impact, knowledge, access, responsibility, influence, and decision authority.
3. Explain the reported, observed, measured, reproduced, and production evidence levels.
4. Why is stakeholder opinion useful evidence but not automatically workflow fact?
5. What is gained and lost when sensitive evidence is inspected but not retained?
6. How does an access register expose architecture and handoff risk?
7. When has discovery produced enough evidence to advance without claiming complete domain knowledge?
8. Why can “representative” require deliberate edge-case inclusion rather than an average user?

## Scenario questions

### The generous sponsor

A sponsor offers a full production export and administrator credentials. Write an access-purpose chain for the exact discovery question, then propose a minimized alternative. Identify approver, handling, expiry, and the decision enabled.

### Conflicting evidence

The data team reports 99.5 percent equipment-ID completeness. Technicians say the identifier is routinely unusable. List at least five explanations that allow both statements to be true. Design the least costly legitimate evidence sequence that distinguishes them.

### Missing users

The project can interview senior technicians at headquarters but cannot reach contractors, night-shift workers, or low-connectivity sites before the planning deadline. What may the team conclude, what must remain a gap, and how should scope/rollout contain the missing evidence?

### Security says no

A platform engineer reports that “security will not allow” the proposed integration. Rewrite that report into a set of decision-quality questions without dismissing the concern or accepting it as a complete formal decision.

## Applied exercise - discovery decision packet

For a fictional workflow, produce:

- five decisions discovery must enable;
- a stakeholder map using all six dimensions;
- three interview prompts and two observation prompts;
- an access register with granted, pending, rejected, and expiring items;
- five evidence-log entries across at least three levels;
- two contradictions with next tests;
- missing voices and consequences;
- stop/escalation conditions;
- a one-page next-decision packet.

Pass only when no access item exists merely “in case,” every quantitative statement has population/definition limits, and contradictions remain visible.

## Optional practice MCQs

### 1. A technician says, “The inventory API is always stale.” What is the strongest immediate conclusion?

A. The API must be replaced.  
B. The technician reports experiencing or believing the API is stale; context, semantics, frequency, and impact require further evidence.  
C. Inventory data is unsafe for every workflow.  
D. The statement should be ignored because it is not measured.

Answer: **B**.

### 2. Which access record is strongest?

A. “Production access for discovery, no expiry.”  
B. “Read-only because writes are dangerous.”  
C. “Customer analyst runs versioned query Q-17 for selected-family mismatch classes; FDE receives reviewed aggregate; owner, handling, and decision are recorded; expires after validation.”  
D. “Admin access approved verbally by the sponsor.”

Answer: **C**.

### 3. Which statement best describes observation?

A. It proves population frequency.  
B. It reveals behavior under stated conditions and can generate or test hypotheses, but sampling and observer effects remain limitations.  
C. It replaces interviews and event evidence.  
D. It transfers domain authority to the observer.

Answer: **B**.

## Advanced challenge - minimal evidence design

You must decide whether a safety-relevant exception belongs in the first slice. Raw production data cannot leave the customer environment, observation access is limited, and policy language is ambiguous. Design a staged evidence plan that includes a qualified owner, customer-controlled analysis, redacted or synthetic reproduction, explicit uncertainty, and a stop rule. Explain why each step is necessary and what it still cannot prove.
