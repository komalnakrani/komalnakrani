## Conceptual questions

1. Why is an outcome contract not a commercial contract or feature specification?
2. Distinguish outcome, leading workflow, adoption/use, system/quality, and guardrail measures.
3. What fields turn a metric label into a metric contract?
4. Why must baseline comparability be tested rather than assumed?
5. Explain the attribution ladder and the evidence required at each level.
6. Why can active use be necessary but insufficient evidence?
7. How does a guardrail become actionable rather than decorative?
8. Why should a result-decision table be written before the result exists?

## Scenario questions

### Faster closure

Ticket closure improves, but repeat visits and transfers rise. Show how workflow states, denominator changes, and guardrails affect the claim. Which evidence may be reported, and which success claim is prohibited?

### High use

Ninety percent of the exposed cohort uses the tool, but only half of eligible cases are exposed and bypass reasons are missing. Build the complete population funnel and identify the misleading headline.

### Critical segment

Aggregate evidence-retrieval quality is acceptable, but one safety-relevant equipment subfamily performs poorly. Write the decision and authority path; do not allow the average to override consequence.

### No baseline

The current workflow lacks reliable start/end events. Propose a prospective baseline, a weaker claim, and a stop rule. State which causal language remains unavailable.

## Applied exercise - `OA-03`

Using the workflow from Chapter 3, produce:

- bounded outcome hypothesis and population;
- metric tree with at least two measures in each branch;
- exact definitions for one outcome, one adoption, and one guardrail measure;
- baseline plan and comparability risks;
- critical segments and denominator flow;
- gaming tests;
- attribution level and limitations;
- guardrail control actions;
- assumption/constraint/decision records;
- recommend/approve/execute/inform map;
- non-goals, rejection, entrance, exit, and stop criteria;
- evidence-state decision table.

Pass only when every measure can change a named decision and no missing target/threshold has been invented.

## Optional practice MCQs

### 1. Which is the strongest outcome statement at the contract stage?

A. Launch the copilot by the seasonal peak.  
B. Increase prompts per technician.  
C. For the bounded cohort and case definition, test whether the evidence-assisted path changes supported-resolution evidence within named guardrails, definitions, owners, and attribution limits.  
D. Achieve industry-leading AI accuracy.

Answer: **C**.

### 2. A guardrail is measured but has no owner or action. What is it?

A. Complete governance.  
B. Observation without an operational decision path.  
C. A production block in every context.  
D. Proof of safety.

Answer: **B**.

### 3. Which result supports the strongest causal claim?

A. A favorable demo.  
B. Before/after movement during a seasonal change.  
C. A study design appropriate to the claim with credible assignment/comparison, definitions, assumptions, and specialist review.  
D. High active use.

Answer: **C**.
