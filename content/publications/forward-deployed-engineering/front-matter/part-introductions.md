# Part Introductions

## Part I - Own the Outcome

The book begins before architecture or code because the first production risk is often a mistaken unit of work. A requested feature can be delivered accurately while the real workflow stays slow, unsafe, unsupported, or unused. Part I teaches the reader to replace feature certainty with an evidence-bearing deployment contract.

Chapter 1 establishes the FDE's unit of accountability and authority boundary. Chapter 2 enters the customer system through purposeful evidence and legitimate access. Chapter 3 maps real decisions, states, handoffs, exceptions, incentives, and failure cost. Chapter 4 turns that evidence into outcomes, guardrails, adoption signals, decision rights, and stop/exit criteria. Chapter 5 selects the least exposed representative production path and sequences uncertainty before irreversible commitment.

The Orchid dossier advances from `OA-01` role/discovery through `OA-04` scope/delivery. At the end of the part, the reader should be able to reject a broad `AI copilot` request, a metric without a denominator, a scope that hides unsafe dependency, or an engagement whose authority and exit are undefined.

## Part II - Design the Deployment

Part II converts the selected workflow into explicit boundaries that reviewers can challenge. Boxes alone are not architecture. The deployment must state which system holds which responsibility, which proposition crosses each interface, how identities and regions are enforced, where uncertain model behavior belongs, and which control evidence supports formal decisions.

Chapter 6 draws structural, runtime, data, trust, failure, and responsibility boundaries. Chapter 7 makes interface semantics, time, intent, finality, error, quality, lineage, and reconciliation explicit. Chapter 8 instantiates the logical design in customer topology, identity, network, configuration, secret, tenancy, regional, capacity, connectivity, and recovery constraints. Chapter 9 permits AI only as a bounded mechanism with task-relevant evaluation, deterministic controls, qualified review, fallback, budgets, and stop behavior. Chapter 10 connects threats and risks to control objectives, implementation, evidence, authority, and expiry.

These chapters complete `OA-05`. Their purpose is not to select fashionable infrastructure or reproduce specialist standards. It is to make the design falsifiable before implementation hides ambiguity behind working code.

## Part III - Build Evidence Into the System

Part III changes the central question from `does it work?` to `which production claims does the current evidence support?`

Chapter 11 builds one small but consequentially complete vertical slice. It preserves truth across configuration, contracts, equipment identity, evidence, external finality, bounded suggestion, deterministic policy, qualified approval, audit, telemetry, failure, and support. Chapter 12 traces workflow risk and acceptance criteria into typed verification, evaluation, security/accessibility review, UAT, simulation, limitations, and release disposition. Chapter 13 carries those claims into promise-linked signals, cohorts, alerts, diagnosis, privacy-safe telemetry, support, capacity, cost, and runbooks. Chapter 14 binds the release identity and evidence to progressive exposure, compatible migration, actual-state recovery choice, tested restore, break-glass, and rehearsal.

The dossier progresses through `OA-06`, `OA-07`, and `OA-08`. The provider-neutral companion is intentionally synthetic: it makes behavior reviewable and reproducible, while preserving the real integration, identity, performance, accessibility, UAT, and production limitations it cannot prove.

## Part IV - Launch Into Reality

Production exposure does not resolve uncertainty. It changes who and what can be affected. Part IV teaches how to cross that boundary with named authority, respond when evidence changes, and leave a system that users and customer operators can actually own.

Chapter 15 assembles the readiness packet and supports go, conditional go, reduced scope, delay, or stop. Cohort, observation, critical-case coverage, triggers, command, support, communication, and recovery are part of the decision. Chapter 16 operates within customer incident command to establish impact, preserve calibrated truth, contain and restore, deliver durable correction, and prove stabilization exit. Chapter 17 diagnoses adoption without blaming users and requires demonstrated customer capability across knowledge, access, telemetry, runbook, release, recovery, support, decisions, open risk, and change.

The dossier advances through `OA-09` and `OA-10`. Handoff is not a folder or meeting. It is a bounded state in which representative users can complete the workflow, customer operators can act without hidden FDE dependence, and remaining exceptions have owners and exits.

## Part V - Turn Delivery Into Leverage

The final part asks what can legitimately travel beyond one engagement. Field experience is valuable, but it becomes product leverage only after customer-specific data, authority, policy, workflow, and implementation are separated from a repeated invariant.

Chapter 18 closes the bounded outcome review, maintains a pattern ledger, classifies artifacts, prices compatibility and maintenance, and writes propose/defer/reject product packets with validation and disconfirmation. Chapter 19 allocates attention across deployments using consequence, evidence gap, reversibility, and decision latency; it delegates with authority and evidence; communicates shared facts at multiple altitudes; converts recurring escalation into system capacity; and plans growth through capability evidence rather than title or burnout.

`OA-11` and the dossier index close the lifecycle. Closure does not mean every uncertainty vanished. It means the canonical source/build, limitations, risk/reuse state, decisions, and next owners are visible and resumable.
