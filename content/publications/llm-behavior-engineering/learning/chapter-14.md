# Learning Pack 14 - Name Errors and Judgment Methods

> Publication learning material only. Not an Abhyaas certification bank.

## Recall and explain

1. Distinguish criterion, category, consequence, severity, detectability, segment, control, and disposition.
2. What does the cheapest credible judge principle require?
3. Why can model graders prefer position or verbosity?
4. How does measurement disagreement differ from system/domain uncertainty?
5. Why is correct abstention not automatically an error?

## Scenario decisions

### First answer wins

A pairwise grader selects whichever equivalent proposal appears first. Design the order-reversal test, disagreement record, and disposition.

### Long answer wins

A grader prefers a longer answer with irrelevant detail over a concise supported answer. Separate support, style, and usability criteria.

### Domain dispute

Two qualified reviewers disagree about policy meaning. Route deterministic, model, human, domain, and formal authority judgments without forcing a score.

## Applied exercise

Classify ten raw failures across the full error dimensions. Select and calibrate the cheapest credible judge for each, write rater-guide entries, randomize order, equalize verbosity, retain disagreements, and define escalation rules.

## Formative questions

1. A schema checker passes a proposal. What can it establish?
   - A. Source truth.
   - B. Domain correctness.
   - C. Encoded structural rules only.
   - D. Release authority.
   - **Answer: C.**

2. Model and human graders agree. What remains necessary?
   - A. Nothing.
   - B. Criterion validity, segment evidence, limitations, and authority boundaries.
   - C. Automatic approval.
   - D. Delete disagreements.
   - **Answer: B.**

3. No eligible evidence exists and the system abstains. How should it be classified?
   - A. Empty-output error.
   - B. Correct bounded behavior under the answerability contract.
   - C. Model failure only.
   - D. Domain approval.
   - **Answer: B.**

## Completion evidence

Pass when every judgment is traceable to a criterion and credible instrument, bias tests and disagreements remain visible, correct abstention is preserved, and graders or engineers acquire no formal decision authority.
