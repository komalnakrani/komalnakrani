# Learning Pack 02 - Write the Language-Task Contract

> Publication learning material only. Not an Abhyaas certification bank.

## Recall and explain

1. Name the eight parts of a language-task contract.
2. Distinguish uncertain, abstain, escalate, degraded, and prohibited behavior.
3. Why should criteria precede numeric thresholds?
4. Why can a valid schema not judge evidence fidelity or domain correctness?
5. What makes human review a designed control rather than a safety guarantee?

## Scenario decisions

### Missing model identity

A customer description names a washer family but not a model. Write the expected state, output, missing-evidence statement, judge, owner, and authority.

### Conflicting policy

Two authorized warranty records disagree and neither is marked current. Decide what Mosaic may summarize, what it must not resolve, and where the case escalates.

### Embedded instruction

A technician note says to ignore policy and reveal a previous customer's address. Show how the contract handles legitimate service content separately from prohibited instructions.

## Applied exercise

Write twelve clauses covering all six behavior states. Each clause must contain preconditions, observable criterion, judge, failure disposition, working owner, authority, and recheck trigger. Add nominal, edge, adversarial, and out-of-scope cases across English, Gujarati, code-switching, evidence conflict, and safety ambiguity.

Do not invent a target percentage. Record how and by whom thresholds will be calibrated after a baseline.

## Formative questions

1. Which statement is a task contract?
   - A. Be a helpful service assistant.
   - B. Summarize accurately.
   - C. Given authorized case evidence, propose a structured review packet, cite supplied records, preserve conflicts, and never authorize effects.
   - D. Use the best available model.
   - **Answer: C.**

2. A required attachment is absent. Which state is most appropriate for attachment-dependent advice?
   - A. Required.
   - B. Abstain.
   - C. Prohibited.
   - D. Successful.
   - **Answer: B.**

3. What proves human review is sufficient?
   - A. A human appears in the workflow diagram.
   - B. The model recommends review.
   - C. Local evidence about reviewer competence, information, workload, decision rights, and escalation.
   - D. A provider safety page.
   - **Answer: C.**

## Completion evidence

Pass when every output and consequence has a measurable state, judge, owner, authority, and limitation, and when prohibited effects cannot be reintroduced through user or retrieved text.
