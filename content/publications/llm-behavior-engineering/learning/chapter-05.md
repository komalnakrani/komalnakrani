# Learning Pack 05 - Establish a Reproducible Baseline

> Publication learning material only. Not an Abhyaas certification bank.

## Recall and explain

1. Name the task, request, model-system, execution, and evidence parts of run identity.
2. Distinguish pinned, changeable, and not-observable fields.
3. Why can temperature zero or a seed not guarantee universal determinism?
4. When are repeated trials required?
5. Which claims can a deterministic test double establish, and which must it refuse?

## Scenario decisions

### Two outputs, one model alias

The messages look identical, but template, adapter, and provider metadata were not retained. Decide which claims are supportable and which evidence must be repaired before comparison.

### A green fixture

All deterministic result states pass locally. A stakeholder calls the model accurate and secure. Identify the category error and list the live or authority evidence still absent.

### Raw traces without policy

A proposed baseline stores technician notes and provider responses indefinitely. Name the privacy, access, retention, and deletion decisions required before non-synthetic traces may be collected.

## Applied exercise

Repair an incomplete experiment log. Add task and case revisions, semantic and rendered message identity, model/access fields, tokenizer/template visibility, decoding, context/tool state, schema, runtime, code, evaluator, raw evidence, limitations, and privacy policy reference. Then:

- replay success, invalid, timeout, auth failure, abstain, and escalate fixtures;
- verify the canonical manifest hash;
- change one behavior-facing field and record the new identity;
- design three future live trials without claiming the count is universally sufficient;
- issue `usable`, `repair`, or `blocked` disposition.

## Formative questions

1. Which record is reproducible enough for controlled comparison?
   - A. A screenshot and model name.
   - B. A prompt and seed.
   - C. A versioned manifest, cases, raw evidence, evaluator, replay instructions, and explicit unknowns.
   - D. One benchmark score.
   - **Answer: C.**

2. A repeated output differs under the same observable configuration. What follows?
   - A. The prompt improved.
   - B. Behavior variation was observed; attribution requires the declared trial design.
   - C. The provider changed the model.
   - D. The result should be discarded.
   - **Answer: B.**

3. What can a deterministic result fixture prove?
   - A. Gujarati task quality.
   - B. Production latency.
   - C. The software preserves explicit success and failure states under the fixture contract.
   - D. Provider determinism.
   - **Answer: C.**

## Completion evidence

Pass when another engineer can reconstruct the intended configuration, reproduce deterministic identities and states, locate evidence and evaluator versions, distinguish variation from change, and name every live-quality or authority claim the baseline does not support.
